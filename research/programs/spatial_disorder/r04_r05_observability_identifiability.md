# R04/R05 observability and identifiability memo

**Controlling issue:** #395  
**Decision scope:** determine whether a proposed evidence package can constrain `m`, `g`, and the convolved correlated-versus-scalar effect

## 1. Inference target

The physical inputs are

\[
\theta=(\overline M,\sigma_M,\xi,\phi_{\rm cov},\phi_{\rm spatial},\phi_{\rm spec}),
\]

where

- `Mbar` is the signed mean mass;
- `sigma_M` is the local mass standard deviation;
- `xi` is the material correlation length;
- `phi_cov` denotes covariance-family parameters;
- `phi_spatial` denotes spatial-instrument, finite-map, and calibration nuisance parameters;
- `phi_spec` denotes spectroscopy-kernel, background, surface, and calibration nuisance parameters.

The derived R05 controls are

\[
g=\frac{\sigma_M\xi}{\hbar v_K},
\qquad
m=\frac{\overline M\xi}{\hbar v_K}.
\]

The evidence package is useful only if the joint posterior constrains these products rather than merely assigning point estimates to separately unidentifiable factors.

## 2. Structural identifiability requirements

### 2.1 Spatial map

A single filtered variance cannot identify both local variance and correlation length. The R04 Gaussian benchmark gives

\[
V(s)=\frac{A}{1+2s^2/\xi^2}.
\]

At least two independently calibrated effective scales are required to estimate `A` and `xi` within the Gaussian family, and at least three are required for the first exact covariance-family closure test. Multiple resolutions derived from the same raster are correlated and must not be treated as independent observations.

The spatial record therefore requires either:

- at least three independently characterized effective scales; or
- a full-resolution map with a known transfer function and sufficient spatial-frequency support to fit the covariance spectrum directly.

A map whose PSF width is unknown has an exact or near-exact confounding between material correlation length and instrument blur.

### 2.2 Mass conversion

Composition is not the final latent quantity. The conversion

\[
M(x,T)=\frac{E_g(x,T)}{2}
\]

must include uncertainty in the gap model, temperature, composition calibration, strain, and confinement where relevant.

For small fluctuations around `x0`,

\[
\sigma_M\simeq\frac12\left|\frac{\partial E_g}{\partial x}\right|_{x_0,T}\sigma_x.
\]

This linearization must be tested against the exact nonlinear transform over the inferred composition distribution. It cannot be applied to a macroscopic tolerance without evidence that the tolerance represents the local fluctuation distribution.

### 2.3 Spectroscopy

The measured spectrum is represented schematically as

\[
y(E)=\mathcal B\left[
K_E * \rho(E;\theta)
\right]+\epsilon,
\]

where `K_E` is the measured energy-resolution kernel and `B` includes background, normalization, surface, contact, and band-bending effects.

The correlated and scalar models are not distinguishable when the nuisance subspace generated by `B` can reproduce their difference. The analysis must therefore report the component of the model difference orthogonal to the nuisance tangent space or an equivalent posterior model-comparison diagnostic.

## 3. Practical observability gates

### 3.1 Spatial transfer-band gate

Let `H_spatial(k)` be the complete spatial transfer function, including PSF, pixel integration, depth weighting, and preprocessing. The material covariance is observable only over the band where

\[
|H_{\rm spatial}(k)|^2 S_M(k)
\]

is above the calibrated noise floor and the map window supplies enough independent modes.

The correlation-length gate fails if the posterior for `xi` is dominated by the prior or lies outside the supported transfer band.

### 3.2 Same-population gate

A spatial map and a spectrum taken from different pieces cannot be combined by default. The exchangeability model must quantify expected between-region or between-specimen variance.

Acceptable levels, strongest first:

1. same coordinates before and after nondestructive measurements;
2. same specimen, registered nearby regions with an explicit spatial model;
3. adjacent lamella/needle and spectroscopy region with quantified within-wafer covariance;
4. same growth run with a hierarchical between-specimen model;
5. unlinked samples, which fail the gate.

Destructive composition methods can support the gate only through levels 3 or 4 and require a predeclared hierarchy.

### 3.3 Near-critical gate

The R05 benchmark shows increasing suppression as `|m|` increases. The evidence protocol must freeze `m_max` before final model comparison. A reasonable PR2 task is to interpolate the immutable R05 detuning screen and select `m_max` from a predeclared minimum effect-retention fraction.

No numerical value is frozen in PR1 because the acceptable retention fraction is a scientific decision that must be justified explicitly rather than inferred from the observed data.

### 3.4 Resolution gate

The experimental kernel must be measured or inferred from calibration data. Reporting only temperature or lock-in amplitude is insufficient because wiring noise, filtering, modulation waveform, tip state, and environmental broadening can dominate.

The gate passes only if the posterior predictive correlated-versus-scalar difference exceeds the predeclared effect threshold after applying the measured kernel and nuisance model.

### 3.5 Matched-null gate

The scalar null must use the same one-point mass distribution as the correlated model. Allowing different marginals would convert the test into a mixture of correlation and distributional effects.

The comparison must therefore share:

- `P(M)`;
- mass/gap conversion;
- spectroscopy kernel;
- background and normalization conventions;
- energy window;
- uncertainty treatment.

Only the spatial organization of the mass field may differ in the primary test.

## 4. Required uncertainty decomposition

Report separately:

- finite-map statistical uncertainty;
- repeated-map or repeated-spectrum variability;
- spatial calibration uncertainty;
- PSF/pixel/depth-kernel uncertainty;
- composition/gap conversion uncertainty;
- covariance-family uncertainty;
- energy-axis and energy-kernel uncertainty;
- surface/contact/band-bending uncertainty;
- background-model uncertainty;
- specimen-exchangeability uncertainty;
- numerical surrogate or interpolation uncertainty.

A single aggregate standard error is insufficient because several of these terms create directional biases that can mimic the model difference.

## 5. Falsification checks

The joint analysis must include at minimum:

1. covariance-family replacement: Gaussian versus Matérn alternatives;
2. spatial-window and region holdout;
3. PSF and pixel-kernel perturbation within calibration uncertainty;
4. exact versus linearized composition-to-mass conversion;
5. spectroscopy background alternatives;
6. energy-kernel alternatives supported by calibration;
7. surface-state or band-bending nuisance expansion;
8. same-specimen versus hierarchical exchangeability variants;
9. prior sensitivity for `sigma_M` and `xi`;
10. scalar-null recovery on synthetic data generated from the scalar model;
11. correlated-model recovery on synthetic data generated from the correlated model;
12. false-positive calibration under the complete decision rule.

Synthetic recovery validates the inference procedure only. It does not validate HgCdTe random-mass physics.

## 6. Decision statistics

The primary posterior quantities are

\[
P(g>0.25\mid D)
\]

and

\[
P(|m|<m_{\max}\mid D).
\]

The model distinction should be reported through at least one predictive effect metric and one likelihood-based or predictive-score metric. Candidate quantities include:

- posterior distribution of the convolved `Delta_1` metric used in R05;
- held-out log predictive density difference;
- likelihood-ratio statistic calibrated under the scalar null;
- Bayes factor, only with declared prior sensitivity;
- posterior probability that the correlated model exceeds the frozen practical-effect threshold.

No single metric should be interpreted without the falsification variants above.

## 7. Minimal viable experiment

The minimal credible design is not one map plus one averaged spectrum. It is:

- one near-critical specimen with a registered region plan;
- at least three calibrated spatial scales or a transfer-function-resolved map;
- multiple independent map regions or a full finite-map covariance treatment;
- local composition or signed-gap calibration;
- cryogenic spatially indexed STS with a measured energy kernel;
- repeated spectra and surface-state controls;
- destructive STEM-EDX or APT only after nondestructive registration, with an exchangeability model;
- a predeclared scalar-null calibration and stop rule.

## 8. PR1 conclusion

The parameter products are structurally observable in principle, but only under a deliberately matched experiment. Existing R04 and R05 infrastructure is sufficient to define the inference and stopping rules. The dominant risk is external evidence quality, especially same-population linkage and HgCdTe surface-systematics control.
