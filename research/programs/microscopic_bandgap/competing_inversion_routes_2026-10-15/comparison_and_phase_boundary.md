## Real-axis SCBA mass is a competing model criterion, not an atom assertion

Krishtopenko2022 uses a six-state heavy-hole-inclusive SCBA with real
renormalized common/mass quantities and tests DOS/dispersion. Its own
non-Lorentzian spectral discussion is compatible with a useful model mass
without perfectly coherent quasiparticles. We therefore do NOT reject that
criterion merely because a delta atom is absent. The distinct question is
whether a real material optical/mobility/phase observable is boundedly related
to that chosen mass. The paper's independent scalar/mass Gaussian model and
parameter lineage do not provide the missing atomistic chemical covariance.
It does not compute a disorder optical vertex that can be imported here.

For an exact total projected inverse D6=F_s-m+i Gamma6 and
D8=F_s+m+i Gamma8, a real common-root construction is legitimate when boundary
values, branch tracking, common-root existence/uniqueness and errors are
certified. Its zero can remain a defined real-inverse crossover with finite
damping; it is not automatically a coincident complex resonance, spectral
maximum or sharp excitation gap. The earlier finite-eta/tangency/Green-zero
counterexamples prohibit those replacements. A generic energy-dependent
self-energy need not preserve the fixed a,M of the optical spectrum across
fields. RouteB's third-field/remainder gate tests a restricted adapter rather
than declaring every SCBA real mass experimentally identical.

## A rigorously identifiable phase statement without averaged atoms

Established homotopy/perturbation argument, **PROVEN UNDER ASSUMPTIONS**:
suppose H_K(x) is a local, globally defined, TR-symmetric regularized electronic
Hamiltonian at fixed filling, and its Fermi level lies a distance g_K(x)>0
from its spectrum. Suppose EACH realization has a TR-preserving bounded
perturbation ||W_omega(x)||<=w(x)<g_K(x), with a common physical limit and
the locality conditions for the intended index. Then

    H_t=H_K+t W_omega,  0<=t<=1,
    dist(mu,spec H_t)>=g_K-tw>0.

The occupied projector has a continuous symmetry-preserving homotopy, so its
insulating topological index equals that of H_K. This needs no delta atom in
an averaged spectrum and no support-free *eliminated* domain D. It DOES require
a true sample/global gap (or a separately proved mobility-gap replacement).
It is not a new topological theorem.

In a restricted gapped Dirac regularization with a certified only closing at
Gamma and g_K=|M_K|, any possible disorder-induced change relative to that
clean phase is confined to |M_K(x)|<=w(x). Independently known opposite phases
on its sides give a conditional region for at least one transition, not
uniqueness or m_real=0 equivalence within it. For a monotone M_K with lower
slope s and a known clean zero, this uncertainty region is contained within
w_max/s of that zero. No endpoint interpolation or empirical slope is used.

**Not activated for unstrained inverted cubic HgCdTe:** HH touching removes
the required insulating gap, UV/other-momentum structure is not identified,
and w, filling and index hypotheses are not available. Straining/gapping the
system would define a separate physical path, not silently repair this one.
A mobility gap can tolerate localized spectral weight, but proving it needs
localization/real-space information absent from a Gamma averaged self-energy.
Arithmetic-average Green zeros can also differ from sample topology. The
earlier Gamma-identical/different-global-topology construction remains decisive.

The common-scalar example in routeB provides a complementary exact point:
at canonical filling each realization's projector and any well-defined clean
index are unchanged by C_omega I, while its ARITHMETIC common-energy averaged
spectrum can be entirely continuous. Thus atom applicability and topological
stability are logically separate; neither proves the other's material gates.
