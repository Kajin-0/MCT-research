## What was tested

A one-dimensional two-component random-mass Dirac Hamiltonian was compared against an incoherent scalar mixture using the identical Gaussian one-point mass distribution.

The implementation tested:

- Fourier-pseudospectral Hermitian propagation;
- exact homogeneous finite-box spectra;
- exact and quadrature scalar-null references;
- Gaussian and Matérn correlated fields;
- coherent sign-changing-wall states;
- periodic and antiperiodic boundaries;
- disorder sampling;
- finite size and grid spacing;
- numerical broadening;
- finite-box field conditioning;
- the frozen experimental-resolution convolution and effect-size criterion.

## Why it was tested

The PR 1 audit established that generic correlation-dependent random-mass DOS physics is prior art. The numerical oracle was therefore designed only to determine whether a matched correlated-versus-scalar distinction exists in a controlled model and what minimum regime would merit HgCdTe physical screening.

## Assumptions

```text
spatial dimension = 1
two-component symmetric Dirac model
M = Eg/2
constant v_K
Gaussian primary marginal
finite-range Gaussian primary covariance
DOS per length per two-component species
no heavy-hole, split-off, optical, magnetic, or electron-hole-asymmetry physics
```

## Failed tests and negative findings

No declared numerical gate failed.

Important negative findings remain:

1. the feature is not robust to broad experimental convolution; it falls below the 10% threshold by dimensionless width `0.50`;
2. the calculation is one-dimensional and cannot establish the bulk HgCdTe DOS;
3. the repository still lacks a qualifying near-critical HgCdTe mass-correlation length;
4. the calculation does not identify an experiment or justify full Kane production work.

## Remaining uncertainty

Statistical uncertainty is smaller than the numerical-systematic envelope. The dominant declared drifts are finite size and numerical broadening, not seed sampling.

The larger unresolved uncertainty is physical rather than numerical:

- whether real HgCdTe reaches `g approximately 0.3`;
- whether the 1D coherent-wall mechanism survives in 3D Kane structure;
- whether a realistic measurement can resolve a feature narrower than the required convolution threshold;
- whether heavy-hole and multiband disorder vertices suppress, enhance, or qualitatively alter the effect.

## Claims supported

## Claims not supported
